# Loss-function experiment findings

Generated by `scripts/loss_analysis.py`, which was written, smoke-tested on
synthetic data and fixed before launch, from the per-sample prediction CSVs
in this folder (`<loss>_seed<s>_h3.csv`, `<loss>_seed<s>_h6.csv`) and the
training logs in `logs/loss/`. Per-run numbers in `summary_per_run.csv`,
per-arm tables in `summary_by_arm_h3.csv` and `summary_by_arm_h6.csv`, the
six primary tests in `primary_tests.csv`, everything else in
`exploratory_differences.csv`. Runs made by `scripts/loss_experiment.sh`.
Design, approval and results entries in `logs/decisions.md` (2026-10-14).
Only each run's validation-selected checkpoint was kept
(`models/loss/<loss>/seed<s>.pt`). `best_model.pt` and
`models/baseline_9kt.pt` were not touched.

## Hypothesis

L1 optimizes the conditional median, which biases predictions low in the
right tail of a skewed label distribution. If that drives the
underestimation above 96 kt, training with Huber or MSE should reduce it
on the same data.

## Verdict: not supported -- the effect reversed

Both alternative losses make the high-end underestimation *worse*, not
better. On the six-storm held-out set, >96 kt bias goes from -14.5 kt (L1)
to -16.7 (Huber) and -18.3 (MSE). The three MSE primary tests survive
correction, all in the worse direction. Huber's >96 kt bias result sits on
the threshold. No test in either arm shows an improvement.

## Design

Three arms, ten seeds each (0-9), 30 runs. Only the loss differs.

| arm | loss |
|---|---|
| L1 (control) | L1, as in every earlier run |
| Huber | Huber, delta = 10 kt (10/185 in label units), fixed before launch, not tuned |
| MSE | mean squared error |

Same in every arm: the original nine training storms
(`storms_train_baseline.txt`, 1307 frames), validation storms Sally,
Ophelia and Laura (386 frames), ResNet-18 IMAGENET1K_V1, Adam lr 1e-4,
batch 32, 40 epochs. Every arm selects its checkpoint on validation MAE in
knots, not on its own loss. Runs went round-robin by seed with the arm
order rotated each round (L/H/M, H/M/L, M/L/H).

Every run was evaluated on two held-out sets. None of the six storms is in
training or validation.

| set | storms | frames | >96 kt frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| H3 | Ian, Nicole, Claudette | 393 | 47 (Ian) |
| H6 | H3 + Beryl, Lee, Sam | 1197 | 454 (Ian 47, Beryl 104, Lee 115, Sam 188) |

H6 has 31 Cat 5 frames (Ian 4, Beryl 18, Lee 9) where H3 has 4.

## Primary tests

Named before launch: on H6 pooled, >96 kt bias, >96 kt MAE and overall MAE,
for Huber - L1 and MSE - L1. Six tests, two-sided, Bonferroni threshold
0.05 / 6 = 0.0083. The pre-registered test is a permutation test on the
difference of arm means with 40,000 permutations; the exact version of the
same test, enumerating all 184,756 splits of the twenty runs, is shown
alongside.

| test | L1 | arm | difference | p (40,000 perms) | exact p | survives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >96 kt bias, Huber - L1 | -14.46 | -16.66 | -2.20 | 0.0078 | 0.0084 | on the threshold |
| >96 kt MAE, Huber - L1 | 17.04 | 18.40 | +1.37 | 0.027 | 0.027 | no |
| overall MAE, Huber - L1 | 12.20 | 12.85 | +0.65 | 0.033 | 0.034 | no |
| >96 kt bias, MSE - L1 | -14.46 | -18.25 | -3.79 | < 0.0001 | 0.00004 | yes (worse) |
| >96 kt MAE, MSE - L1 | 17.04 | 20.05 | +3.02 | < 0.0001 | 0.00005 | yes (worse) |
| overall MAE, MSE - L1 | 12.20 | 14.00 | +1.80 | < 0.0001 | 0.00003 | yes (worse) |

The Huber >96 kt bias result is on the threshold: 0.0078 by the
pre-registered procedure, 0.0084 exactly. It is the same test with the
sampling error of the permutation estimate removed, so it is not counted
as a clear survivor. Its direction is worse either way.

## H3 alone would have suggested Huber helps

On the original three held-out storms, Huber looks neutral to helpful:
overall MAE -0.20 (p 0.33), fixes-only -0.33 (p 0.15), and Ian's >96 kt MAE
falls with both Huber (-1.40, p 0.02) and MSE (-1.78, p 0.01). That
"improvement" is Ian's Cat 3 over-prediction shrinking (Cat 3 bias +9.5 ->
+6.7 -> +5.3) while Ian's Cat 4/5 bias does not move. On the three storms
H6 adds, every high-end figure gets worse in both arms. Evaluated on H3
only, this experiment would most likely have been written up as a modest
Huber improvement. Ian is the only H3 storm above 96 kt, and its 47 frames
are not enough to judge the high end. (Exploratory, uncorrected.)

## Cat 3 against Cat 4/5 (exploratory)

Ian is over-predicted at Cat 3 and under-predicted at Cat 4/5, so it shows
whether a loss separates the two bands or moves the high end as a block.

- Under L1, Ian's mean prediction on its Cat 4/5 frames is 3.5 kt *below*
  its prediction on its Cat 3 frames; the best track puts them 24.6 kt
  above. Within Ian's high end, the model does not rank Cat 4/5 above
  Cat 3 at all.
- Huber and MSE lower Ian's Cat 3 predictions (bias shift -2.9, p 0.03;
  -4.2, p < 0.01) and leave Cat 4/5 where it was (-0.10, p 0.95; +0.19,
  p 0.93). The predicted gap widens (+2.8, +4.4 kt) only from the Cat 3
  end.
- On Beryl and Sam both bands move down together. On Lee, Cat 4/5 falls
  further than Cat 3 (MSE -8.3 against -0.9), so the gap narrows.

Neither loss raises any storm's Cat 4/5 predictions.

## Interpretation, not a test result

L1 targets the median of the label given the image, MSE targets the mean,
and Huber falls between. Which one predicts higher depends on the shape of
that conditional distribution, not on the skew of the labels overall. If,
for images that look like strong storms, the labels that go with them are
skewed *downward* -- mostly high, with a tail of weaker storms that look
similar -- then the mean sits below the median, and a mean-seeking loss
pulls predictions further down than L1 does. That would produce exactly
the ordering seen above: L1 highest, Huber next, MSE lowest, in all but
one high-end band of Beryl, Lee and Sam. The original hypothesis assumed the
relevant skew was the right tail of the overall label distribution; the
result says the skew that matters runs the other way, image by image.

This is a reading of the result. Nothing in this experiment measures the
conditional distribution directly, and Ian's Cat 4/5 frames, which did not
move in either arm, do not fit it cleanly.

A later check did not support it. In one L1 checkpoint's embedding, the
labels of each held-out high-end frame's 20 nearest training frames are a
local sample of that distribution, and their median minus mean is under
1 kt with mixed sign (Cat 3 -0.02, Cat 4 -0.66, Cat 5 +0.41) -- no
consistent downward skew, and far too small to account for MSE's -3.8 kt
shift. That is the L1 model's embedding, not the MSE model's. Details in
`results/embedding_neighbours/` and `logs/decisions.md` (2026-10-14,
"Embedding-neighbour test").

## Checks

- The L1 arm repeats arm A of the class-imbalance experiment: H3 overall
  9.46 (0.45) against A's 9.39 (0.26), p 0.69. Consistent.
- Run order: no trend across rounds pooled over arms (H3 Spearman -0.15,
  p 0.42; H6 -0.20, p 0.29), and no effect of position within a round. One
  single-arm trend (Huber on H6, -0.66) is read as chance, by the rule
  fixed before launch.
- Integrity: every arm trained on the intended storms, frames and epochs
  with the intended loss; no NaN in any log; the H3 rows of each H6 CSV
  match the H3 CSV to 0.017 kt.

## Limitations

- One architecture, one optimizer setting. The learning rate was not
  retuned per loss (Adam's per-parameter scaling makes the step size
  comparable, but a per-loss sweep could change the picture).
- The Huber delta was fixed at 10 kt before launch; other deltas were not
  tried, by design.
- Checkpoints are selected on validation MAE, which favours L1's own
  objective. Selecting on each arm's loss was ruled out in advance so all
  arms pick epochs by one rule.
