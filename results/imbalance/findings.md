# Class-imbalance experiment findings

Generated by `scripts/imbalance_analysis.py` and `scripts/imbalance_extra.py`
from the per-sample prediction CSVs in this folder (`<arm>_seed<s>_holdout3.csv`,
`<arm>_seed<s>_holdout4.csv`) and the training logs in `logs/imbalance/`.
Per-run numbers in `summary_per_run.csv`, per-arm tables in
`summary_by_arm_h3.csv` and `summary_by_arm_h4.csv`, every pairwise
comparison in `arm_differences.csv`. Per-epoch numbers for all 30 runs in
`epoch_trace.csv` and `heldout_predictions_by_epoch.csv.gz`
(`scripts/imbalance_epoch_trace.py`); the checkpoints themselves were
deleted afterwards. Design written before launch in `logs/decisions.md`
(2026-10-13, 22:10). `best_model.pt` was not touched.

## Hypothesis

The published model underestimates strong storms: on the three held-out
storms, Ian's category 4 and 5 frames come out roughly 18-19 kt low. The
hypothesis was that this is caused by scarcity of Cat 4/5 frames in
training -- the model rarely saw a major hurricane, so it hedges toward
the middle of the distribution it did see. Test: add two Cat 5 storms,
Beryl (AL022024) and Lee (AL132023), to training and see whether the
underestimation above 96 kt shrinks.

## Verdict: not supported

Underestimation above 96 kt did not shrink on Ian in either arm. The only
results that survive correction for multiple comparisons are Ian's >96 kt
MAE getting *worse* in both arms. Nothing improved at the corrected
threshold. But see the next section: the test was weaker than designed,
so this does not rule the hypothesis out either.

## Why the test was weaker than designed

The loader's training frames by band, before and after adding Beryl and
Lee:

| band | original nine storms | eleven storms | added |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat 3 (96-112 kt) | 84 | 212 | +128 (Lee 89, Beryl 39) |
| Cat 4 (113-136 kt) | 108 | 172 | +64 |
| Cat 5 (137+ kt) | 66 | 93 | +27 |
| all >96 kt | 258 (19.7%) | 477 (26.3%) | +219 |

219 frames were added above 96 kt, but 128 of them were Cat 3, mostly
from Lee's long Cat 3 phase. Cat 5's share of training went from 5.0% to
5.1%; Cat 4's from 8.3% to 9.5%. The Cat 4/5 scarcity the hypothesis
blames was barely changed. This is closer to a test of adding Cat 3 data
than a test of fixing Cat 4/5 scarcity.

## Design

Three arms, ten seeds each (0-9), run interleaved by seed. Same model,
loss, optimizer, batch size, validation storms (Sally, Ophelia, Laura) and
preprocessing in every arm; checkpoint = best validation MAE within the
arm's budget.

| arm | training storms | frames | epochs | steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | original nine | 1307 | 40 | 1640 |
| B | eleven (+ Beryl, Lee) | 1816 | 29 | 1653 (equal steps) |
| C | eleven | 1816 | 40 | 2280 (equal epochs) |

Every selected checkpoint was evaluated on the original three held-out
storms (Ian, Nicole, Claudette; 393 frames) and on four (adds Sam,
AL182021; 688 frames). Sam is in no training or validation list. Arms are
compared as independent samples of ten: permutation test on the
difference of means.

## What survived correction

The design named the >96 kt band as the target: >96 kt MAE and bias, on
two held-out sets, for three arm pairs -- 12 primary tests. Bonferroni
threshold 0.05 / 12, about 0.004.

| comparison | difference | permutation p |
|---|---|---|
| Ian >96 kt MAE, B - A | +2.44 kt (worse) | 0.0005 |
| Ian >96 kt MAE, C - A | +3.14 kt (worse) | < 0.0001 |

All ten C runs are worse on this metric than A's worst run. Nothing
improved at the corrected threshold.

## What did not survive

Not significant after correction:

| comparison | difference | permutation p |
|---|---|---|
| Sam >96 kt bias, C - A | +3.66 kt | 0.021 |
| 4-storm >96 kt bias, C - A | +3.30 kt | 0.034 |
| Sam >96 kt MAE, C - A | -1.88 kt | 0.056 |
| 4-storm >96 kt MAE, C - A | -0.88 kt | 0.27 |
| Sam >96 kt bias, B - A | +1.38 kt | 0.22 |
| Sam >96 kt MAE, B - A | -0.85 kt | 0.38 |

Everything else in `arm_differences.csv` (about 100 comparisons, all
bands, both sets) is exploratory and uncorrected.

## Mechanism: most likely reading, not established

Arm C appears to have shifted high-end predictions upward as a block
rather than learning to discriminate within the high end. Sam is
under-predicted throughout above 96 kt (Cat 3 and Cat 4 both ~15 kt low in
A), so a uniform upward shift helps it: Sam's Cat 3 bias moves +4.0 and
its Cat 4 bias +3.5. Ian is over-predicted at Cat 3 and under-predicted at
Cat 4/5, so the same shift makes its Cat 3 worse: Ian Cat 3 bias +8.1 ->
+12.4, Cat 3 MAE 10.8 -> 15.5 (p 0.002, exploratory). That accounts for
most of C's Ian increase: weighting each band by its frame count (26/17/4
of 47), C - A = +3.14 is Cat 3 +2.60, Cat 4 +0.30, Cat 5 +0.25.

Two things that do not fit a clean block shift. Ian's own Cat 4/5 frames
did not move up in C (Cat 4 -0.8, Cat 5 -2.9, neither significant), so
the shift reached Ian's Cat 3 frames and Sam's Cat 4 frames but not Ian's
peak. And arm B, at equal steps, raised Ian's >96 kt MAE a different way:
B - A = +2.44 is Cat 3 +0.80, Cat 4 +1.24, Cat 5 +0.39, mostly Cat 4/5
under-prediction getting deeper (Cat 4 -18.9 -> -22.3, p 0.10; Cat 5
-21.9 -> -26.5, p 0.06). Neither arm separated Cat 4/5 from Cat 3. That
fits an addition that is mostly Cat 3 and leaves the Cat 5 share where it
was.

## Overall MAE

Mean (sd) [range] over ten runs.

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-storm overall | 9.39 (0.26) [8.87-9.71] | 10.02 (0.46) [9.25-10.70] | 9.93 (0.62) [9.07-10.83] |
| 3-storm fixes only | 10.12 (0.47) [9.15-10.75] | 10.79 (0.49) [10.06-11.55] | 10.76 (0.61) [9.99-11.75] |
| 4-storm overall | 11.45 (0.69) [10.55-12.73] | 11.63 (0.83) [10.37-13.16] | 11.34 (0.93) [10.09-13.19] |
| 4-storm fixes only | 11.54 (0.75) [10.32-12.56] | 11.79 (0.76) [10.71-13.15] | 11.49 (0.95) [10.10-13.39] |

On the original three storms the expanded data did worse: B - A overall
+0.62 (p 0.002), fixes +0.67 (p 0.005); C - A overall +0.54 (p 0.022),
fixes +0.64 (p 0.018). On four storms the largest difference is 0.30 kt (fixes, C - B).
Validation MAE is also worse in both expanded arms (A 8.24, B 8.82, C
8.64); it is not used for the comparison, because between runs it does
not predict held-out MAE.

Equal steps (B) against equal epochs (C): indistinguishable overall on
both sets (C - B -0.08 and -0.29). B's validation picks fell at epochs
19-29, seven of ten at 24 or later, so B may be short of converged, but 40
epochs bought nothing measurable overall. The per-epoch traces say the
same thing more directly, paired within run: in each C run, the checkpoint
validation picks with a 29-epoch budget and with a 40-epoch budget differ
by -0.01 kt on three storms and +0.21 kt on four (means over ten runs; five
runs pick the same checkpoint either way). Held-out MAE averaged over
epochs does keep falling in C (four-storm 11.49 over epochs 26-29, 10.98
over 35-40), but validation selection does not capture it.

## Run-to-run spread

33 baseline reruns at 20 epochs give held-out MAE mean 10.05, sd 0.59,
range 8.55-11.37. Arm A, the same configuration at 40 epochs, gives mean
9.39, sd 0.26, range 8.87-9.71. The published 9.00 kt is one run: one of
the 33 reruns and one of the ten A runs is lower. The 0.59 kt figure is
the yardstick for overall MAE only; for band metrics the arm sds are 1-2
kt (MAE) and 2-4 kt (bias), so each band difference is judged against its
own spread and p value.

## Limitation: run order within arm C

Within arm C, seeds 0, 1 and 2 ran first and are its three worst runs on
three-storm overall MAE (Spearman of MAE against run order -0.75; within A
-0.30, within B -0.07). GPU snapshots show no throttling and C's run
times are flat (135-139 s). Seed and run order are confounded within an
arm -- seed 0 always ran first -- so the two cannot be separated. Dropping
C seeds 0-2 changes no sign, but it halves three-storm C - A overall
(+0.54 -> +0.22), moves four-storm C - A overall from -0.12 to -0.57, and
leaves Ian >96 kt MAE C - A at +2.79. The full-arm numbers are the
primary result; the C - A overall figures should be read with this in
mind.
