# Grad-CAM findings

Generated by `scripts/gradcam.py` (Grad-CAM against `layer4` of the trained
ResNet-18 in `best_model.pt`, backpropagating the scalar wind-speed
prediction). Frames: Michael (AL142018) at 35/112/140 kt, Ian (AL092022,
held out) at 30/85/140 kt. Full numbers in `radial_profile.csv` and
`convection_alignment.csv`.

## RETRACTED: "attribution avoids convection at weak intensity"

An earlier version of this file reported, from two hand-picked weak
frames (Michael 35 kt, Ian 30 kt), that attribution was anti-correlated
with the coldest brightness temperatures -- "the model avoids convection
at weak intensity." That does not survive being tested on the full
held-out population and is retracted below. Do not cite the two-frame
numbers.

## What actually holds: convection alignment at weak vs. strong intensity, full held-out population

`convection_alignment()` run over every held-out frame (all three
HOLDOUT_STORMS -- Ian, Nicole, Claudette -- true fixes and interpolated
hours both included) in two matched bands: `wind_kt < 64` (TD/TS) and
`wind_kt > 96` (Cat 3+). No images rendered; per-frame numbers in
`convection_alignment_survey_0_64.csv` and `convection_alignment_survey_96_999.csv`.

**Weak, wind_kt < 64 -- n=277 (Nicole 110, Ian 93, Claudette 74; 50 true fixes, 227 interpolated)**

| statistic | pearson(CAM, coldness) | IoU vs. its own chance level |
|---|---|---|
| mean | +0.126 | +0.081 |
| std | 0.315 | 0.148 |
| min | -0.541 | -0.142 |
| p25 | -0.124 | -0.039 |
| median | +0.134 | +0.053 |
| p75 | +0.356 | +0.158 |
| max | +0.775 | +0.539 |

35.4% of frames have pearson < 0; 32.9% have IoU below their own chance
level.

**Strong, wind_kt > 96 -- n=47 (Ian only; Nicole and Claudette never reach this range in the held-out set; 12 true fixes, 35 interpolated)**

| statistic | pearson(CAM, coldness) | IoU vs. its own chance level |
|---|---|---|
| mean | +0.629 | +0.367 |
| std | 0.067 | 0.107 |
| min | +0.457 | +0.115 |
| p25 | +0.609 | +0.313 |
| median | +0.648 | +0.382 |
| p75 | +0.667 | +0.455 |
| max | +0.722 | +0.535 |

0% of frames have pearson < 0; 0% have IoU below their own chance level.

**Verdict on the original claim:** the weak-frame correlations do *not*
cluster negative -- the median is mildly positive (+0.134) and the
distribution straddles zero (IQR -0.124 to +0.356, min -0.54 to max
+0.78). The two-frame result (+0.24, -0.30) sat inside this same wide
scatter, not outside it -- it was two draws from a noisy, roughly
zero-centered distribution, not evidence of systematic avoidance. The
finding as originally stated does not stand.

**What the matched comparison does show, robustly:** the *reliability* of
convection alignment itself scales with intensity, not just its mean.
At strong intensity the alignment is tight and consistent (std 0.067,
0% of frames negative or below chance) -- Grad-CAM dependably tracking
the coldest cloud tops is a genuine, replicated property of this model
at Cat 3+. At weak intensity there is no such consistency (std 0.315,
roughly a third of frames negative, roughly a third below chance) --
attribution is sometimes near the coldest convection and sometimes far
from it, with no reliable pattern either way. That inconsistency, not a
directional "avoids convection" claim, is the honest characterization of
weak-intensity attribution. This was flagged as a one-storm caveat below
and has since been checked.

## Does the mature-storm alignment result hold beyond Ian? Yes.

The strong-intensity result above came from Ian alone (no other held-out
storm reaches Cat 3+). Tested on three more Atlantic Cat 3+ storms, added
to `storms_gradcam.txt` for this purpose only (attribution test subjects,
not training or evaluation data -- see the file header; `storms_train.txt`,
`storms_holdout.txt`, `best_model.pt`, `train.py` and `evaluate.py` were
not touched, and none of this is reported as held-out MAE): Beryl (2024,
peak 145kt), Lee (2023, peak 145kt), Sam (2021, peak 135kt). Same
`convection_alignment()` test, wind_kt > 96, both true fixes and
interpolated hours. Per-frame numbers in
`convection_alignment_survey_96_999_gradcam_storms.csv`.

| storm | n | pearson mean | pearson std | pearson median | % pearson < 0 | IoU-vs-chance mean | % IoU below chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beryl | 106 (2 dropped, see below) | +0.494 | 0.188 | +0.553 | 2.8% | +0.243 | 4.8% |
| Lee | 115 | +0.604 | 0.118 | +0.629 | 0.0% | +0.236 | 0.0% |
| Sam | 188 | +0.588 | 0.100 | +0.602 | 0.0% | +0.251 | 0.0% |
| Ian (original) | 47 | +0.629 | 0.067 | +0.648 | 0.0% | +0.367 | 0.0% |
| **pooled, 3 new storms** | **409** | **+0.569** | **0.139** | **+0.601** | **0.7%** | **+0.245** | **1.2%** |

**Verdict: holds, plainly.** All three new storms independently show
strong positive alignment (means +0.49 to +0.60, medians +0.55 to +0.63)
with pearson essentially never negative (0-2.8% of frames) and IoU
essentially never below chance (0-4.8%). This is not an Ian-specific
effect -- it replicates across four storms spanning three different years
and three distinct storm behaviors (Ian's landfalling major, two
non-landfalling open-ocean majors in Lee and Sam, and Beryl's rapid
intensification/Caribbean track).

One real wrinkle: Beryl is the noisiest of the four (std 0.188 vs.
0.10-0.12 for the others, and the only nonzero "below chance" and
"negative" rates among the new storms, though both still small). Two
Beryl frames (2024-07-02 17:00 and 18:00, both near its 135-136kt peak)
were dropped outright: roughly half the crop's pixels are NaN in the raw
`.npz` (52%, with DQF reporting all pixels "good" regardless -- a
projection/footprint edge case in that specific crop, not a bug in this
script), which flattens the CAM and makes the correlation undefined. This
doesn't change the verdict -- Beryl is still strongly positive and
Ian-like on the whole -- but it means "tight like Ian" (std 0.067) is
specifically an Ian property, not a guaranteed property of every Cat 3+
storm; "strongly positive," unlike "tight," does generalize.

## Does land interaction explain Beryl's wider spread? Yes, but not the way "landfall" suggests

Beryl is the only one of the four storms that interacted with land while
above 96kt; Lee and Sam were open-ocean their whole lives, Ian's Cat 3+
stretch was pre-landfall. HURDAT2's own status field doesn't distinguish
this -- Beryl is coded `HU` for the entire >96kt period regardless of
surface below it -- so the split here is by geographic proximity: haversine
distance from each frame's best-track position to the nearest of four
reference points along the track (Grenada/Carriacou 12.5N 61.5W, Jamaica
18.1N 77.3W, Grand Cayman 19.3N 81.4W, Yucatan coast 20.5N 86.9W). These
are hand-picked landmarks, not a coastline dataset -- adequate to test this
one storm's specific track, not a general land mask. Per-frame distances
and pearson values in `beryl_land_proximity.csv`.

**Overall relationship:** correlating that distance directly against
pearson(CAM, coldness) gives **+0.428** (n=104) -- farther from land,
better alignment. Binned:

| distance to nearest reference point | n | pearson mean | pearson std | pearson median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <300km | 51 | +0.405 | 0.211 | +0.494 |
| 300-600km | 29 | +0.562 | 0.127 | +0.562 |
| >=600km | 24 | +0.601 | 0.074 | +0.615 |

The >=600km bin's std (0.074) matches Lee/Sam/Ian's open-water tightness
almost exactly. So yes: land proximity, not just noise, accounts for the
spread in aggregate.

**But it's not landfall itself, and not "any land nearby" uniformly --
it's specifically the Jamaica/Grand Cayman passage.** Splitting the <300km
group into its two actual episodes, at matched intensity so this isn't
just re-deriving the weak-vs-strong result:

| episode | n | wind_kt range | distance range | pearson mean | pearson std |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grenada/Carriacou landfall approach (07-01, incl. the official HURDAT2 `L` record at 15:20) | 17 | 100-135 | 0-294km | +0.562 | **0.039** |
| Jamaica/Grand Cayman close passage (07-03 17:00 - 07-04 11:00, no official landfall record) | 19 | 97-121 | 66-225km | **+0.266** | **0.211** |
| open water (>=600km) | 24 | 98-145 | 627-895km | +0.601 | 0.074 |

The actual, HURDAT2-flagged landfall (tiny islands, Grenada/Carriacou,
~450km^2 combined) shows *no* degradation at all -- alignment there is as
tight as anywhere in the storm's life (std 0.039), despite the eye
passing directly over land. The unflagged close passage south of Jamaica
and near Grand Cayman (substantial landmasses, never triggering a HURDAT2
landfall or coast-approach record because Beryl stayed just offshore)
accounts for essentially all the degradation: mean alignment less than
half of open water, std nearly 3x, and every negative-pearson frame in
Beryl's dataset falls in this window. Wind_kt ranges overlap between the
two episodes, so this isn't simply the intensity effect re-appearing.

**Bears directly on excluding overland frames from training:** filtering
by HURDAT2's own landfall flag would catch the Grenada event (which
didn't need catching) and miss the Jamaica/Cayman event (which did the
actual damage to attribution reliability). A land-interaction exclusion
rule keyed to "official landfall" would filter the wrong frames; one keyed
to actual proximity to a landmass of meaningful size would not. This is
suggestive, not conclusive, from a single storm's single passage -- the
correlation between "near Jamaica" and "weakening trend" isn't fully
separated here (eyewall disruption from dry continental air/reduced
symmetry could in principle cause both the intensity drop and the
attribution noise independent of land per se), so treat "landmass size
matters more than the landfall flag" as a lead worth checking against
other storms, not a settled rule.

## Does alignment degrade during rapid intensification? No -- and the frame that prompted the question wasn't actually an RI frame

The check was prompted by Lee's 2023-09-09T00:00Z frame (open ocean the
whole storm, so land can't explain anything there). First, the correction:
that frame is **not** during RI. Its trailing-24h HURDAT2 wind change is
135kt (09-08 00:00) -> 105kt (09-09 00:00) = **-30kt**, and the leading
24h keeps falling to 85kt by 09-10 00:00 -- this is the relaxation after
Lee's second peak (145kt at 09-08 06:00), not intensification by any
window. Its own alignment (pearson +0.646) was fine anyway, in line with
Lee's other frames -- it isn't an outlier that needs explaining.

Checked the general question regardless, since it's a fair thing to ask
independent of that one frame: 24h trailing HURDAT2 wind change at every
>96kt frame across all four storms (Ian included), RI defined as
delta24_kt >= +30kt, compared against everything else. 5 frames dropped
where t-24h fell outside the storm's tracked period. Per-frame numbers in
`ri_alignment_check.csv`.

| storm | RI n | RI pearson mean | RI std | non-RI n | non-RI pearson mean | non-RI std | mean diff (RI - non-RI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ian | 16 | +0.633 | 0.063 | 28 | +0.629 | 0.074 | +0.004 |
| Beryl | 27 | +0.610 | 0.088 | 75 | +0.449 | 0.197 | +0.161 |
| Lee | 25 | +0.640 | 0.083 | 90 | +0.594 | 0.124 | +0.046 |
| Sam | 27 | +0.595 | 0.102 | 161 | +0.587 | 0.099 | +0.008 |
| **pooled** | **95** | **+0.617** | **0.088** | **354** | **+0.563** | **0.143** | **+0.055** |

Continuous version (correlating delta24_kt directly against alignment
pearson): pooled **+0.323**; per storm Beryl +0.597, Lee +0.295, Sam
+0.103, Ian -0.166 (Ian's RI/non-RI means above are essentially identical,
so that's noise in a ~44-frame sample, not a real negative trend).

**Verdict: flat at best, mildly the opposite of the hypothesis --
alignment does not degrade during RI in any of the four storms.** Every
storm's RI mean is equal to or higher than its non-RI mean; none show
degradation. Beryl's gap is the largest (+0.161), but that's not an
independent RI effect -- Beryl's worst frames (the Jamaica/Grand Cayman
passage above) happened during its weakening phase, so they land in the
non-RI bucket and drag that mean down; this is the land-proximity finding
showing up again under a different split, not a second, separate
mechanism. Net: this test gives no support for RI as a driver of
attribution unreliability, in this model, on these four storms.

## At peak intensity, attribution is a filled disk, not a hollow eyewall ring

Radial CAM-mass profile from the crop's geometric (= best-track) center,
in concentric annuli: at 140 kt (both storms, independently), mean CAM is
highest in the innermost annulus (r=0-14px: 0.95-0.96) and falls off
monotonically outward (0.17-0.20 at the corners). A hollow eyewall
signature would instead show a dip at r=0-14 and a peak at some nonzero
radius; that is not what's in the data. Attribution covers the whole
central dense overcast (eye + adjacent eyewall), weighted most heavily
at the eye itself.

## Rendering note

Overlays are Grad-CAM on a 7x7 `layer4` feature map, bilinearly upsampled
to 224x224, then Gaussian-blurred (kernel spans ~32px, one feature-map
cell -- `CAM_BLUR_KERNEL`/`CAM_BLUR_SIGMA` in the script) to remove the
blocky/right-angle artifacts of upsampling a coarse grid. This can only
remove detail, never add it, so it doesn't imply resolution finer than
the method actually has. Default color scheme: plasma, threshold variant
(transparent below CAM 0.5).
