# Rotation-augmentation experiment findings

Generated by `scripts/rotation_analysis.py`, which was written, smoke-tested
on synthetic data and fixed before launch, from the per-sample prediction
CSVs in this folder (`<arm>_seed<s>_h6.csv`), the per-run storm-fraction
files (`<arm>_seed<s>_storm_fraction.json`, from `scripts/storm_fraction.py`)
and the training logs in `logs/rotation/`. Per-run numbers in
`summary_per_run.csv`, per-arm tables in `summary_by_arm.csv`, the three
primary tests in `primary_tests.csv`, everything else in
`exploratory_differences.csv`. Runs made by `scripts/rotation_experiment.sh`.
Design, approval, pre-launch checks and results entries in
`logs/decisions.md` (2026-10-14). Only each run's validation-selected
checkpoint was kept (`models/rotation/<arm>/seed<s>.pt`). `best_model.pt`
and `models/baseline_9kt.pt` were not touched.

## Result

Rotation reduced the >96 kt underestimation on the six held-out storms:
>96 kt bias -13.93 kt (masked control) -> -9.25 kt (rotation), a difference
of **+4.67 kt, exact p 0.0028**, against a corrected threshold of 0.0167.
It is the first intervention in this series to survive correction in the
improving direction.

Stated plainly, the rest:

- The other two primary tests did not survive: >96 kt MAE -2.21 kt
  (p 0.019) and overall MAE -0.49 kt (p 0.25).
- The pre-registered secondary was null. The same-storm neighbour
  fraction barely moved (0.436 -> 0.425, p 0.23), so the improvement did
  not come with the drop in storm clustering the hypothesis predicted.
- Rotation raised predictions at every intensity, not only the high end,
  and made weak storms worse: < 64 kt MAE 7.96 -> 9.90 kt (p < 0.001).
- Ian and H3 went the other way. Ian's >96 kt MAE rose from 15.5 to 18.9
  kt (p < 0.001). On the original three held-out storms overall MAE rose
  from 9.72 to 11.29 kt (p < 0.001).
- Run-to-run sd of H6 overall MAE fell from 1.24 to 0.48 kt.

## Hypothesis

The embedding is partly organized by storm identity, most strongly at Cat
4/5 (`results/embedding_neighbours/`), and held-out strong storms are
placed among moderate training frames. If some of that storm-specific
signal is orientation -- how a particular storm's bands, outflow or
background sit in the frame -- training on randomly rotated frames removes
it and forces features that carry across storms, which should reduce the
>96 kt underestimation on unseen storms.

## Design

Two arms, ten seeds each (0-9), 20 runs.

| arm | circular mask | rotation |
|---|---|---|
| control | train, validation, held-out | none |
| rotation | train, validation, held-out | uniform 0-360 deg per training sample; none at validation or held-out |

- The mask sets everything outside the circle inscribed in the 224 x 224
  frame to 300 K (clear tropical ocean). It is identical in both arms, so
  the only difference is the rotation.
- No flips. A mirrored Northern Hemisphere storm turns the wrong way.
- Same in every run: L1, the original nine training storms (1307 frames),
  validation storms Sally, Ophelia and Laura (386 frames), ResNet-18
  IMAGENET1K_V1, Adam lr 1e-4, batch 32, 40 epochs, checkpoint selected on
  validation MAE.
- Run order: round-robin by seed, arm order alternating each round.

Pre-launch checks, with no training before them:
- Unrotated frames go through the existing resize and are bit-identical
  to it inside the circle (2890/2890 frames).
- The rotation path is a proper rotation, not a reflection.
- Rendered frames were checked by eye.
- The secondary metric reproduced the earlier storm-clustering value
  (0.367).

Held-out set: H6, the six storms Ian, Nicole, Claudette, Beryl, Lee and
Sam. 1197 frames, 454 above 96 kt, masked and unrotated.

## Primary tests

Named before launch: on H6 pooled, rotation - control. Two-sided exact
permutation test on the difference of arm means, enumerating all 184,756
splits of the twenty runs. Bonferroni threshold 0.05 / 3 = 0.0167.

| test | control | rotation | difference | exact p | survives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >96 kt bias | -13.93 (3.52) | -9.25 (2.45) | +4.67 | 0.0028 | yes |
| >96 kt MAE | 16.87 (2.39) | 14.67 (1.25) | -2.21 | 0.019 | no |
| overall MAE | 12.32 (1.24) | 11.83 (0.48) | -0.49 | 0.25 | no |

Mean (sd) over ten runs.

## Pre-registered secondary: null

The same-storm neighbour fraction (k = 20 nearest training frames,
excluding same-storm frames within +-6 h; one value per run) went from
0.436 to 0.425, a difference of -0.011, exact p 0.23. Chance levels depend
only on labels and timing, so they are identical in both arms (0.147 by
storm share, 0.204 matched on intensity).

Exploratory and uncorrected, the fraction drops most at the high end: Cat
4 0.51 -> 0.46 (p 0.03), Cat 5 0.82 -> 0.75 (p 0.02).

## Rotation raised predictions everywhere (exploratory)

Mean bias by band on H6, control -> rotation:

| band | control | rotation | shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| TD | +5.0 | +7.0 | +2.0 |
| TS | -3.6 | -0.9 | +2.7 |
| Cat 1 | -11.1 | -7.5 | +3.6 |
| Cat 2 | -8.3 | -3.2 | +5.2 |
| Cat 3 | -8.3 | -4.7 | +3.6 |
| Cat 4 | -18.4 | -13.1 | +5.3 |
| Cat 5 | -17.7 | -10.4 | +7.3 |

Overall bias -8.2 -> -4.5. The shift is positive in every band and larger
at the high end. Bands that were under-predicted gain; bands that were
over-predicted lose -- < 64 kt MAE 7.96 -> 9.90 kt (p < 0.001), TD bias
+5.0 -> +7.0. At least part of the high-end gain is an upward shift of the
whole output, steeper at the top.

Validation MAE improved (8.37 -> 7.54) and training MAE rose (3.36 ->
5.43), as expected under augmentation. Rotation runs selected epochs 26-40,
3 of 10 at 38 or later, which the pre-set rule does not flag as
under-trained.

## The storms disagree; Ian went the other way (exploratory)

>96 kt, control -> rotation, mean over ten runs:

| storm (>96 kt frames) | MAE | bias |
|---|---|---|
| Beryl (104) | 20.6 -> 17.6 (p 0.005) | -19.1 -> -13.2 (p < 0.001) |
| Lee (115) | 13.4 -> 12.7 (p 0.39) | -10.1 -> -5.4 (p 0.005) |
| Sam (188) | 17.3 -> 13.2 (p 0.003) | -16.2 -> -10.5 (p 0.001) |
| Ian (47) | 15.5 -> 18.9 (p < 0.001) | -2.8 -> -5.2 (p 0.17) |

Ian is over-predicted at Cat 3 and under-predicted at Cat 4/5, so it is
the check on whether rotation separates the bands. It does not:
- Ian's Cat 3 bias is unchanged (-0.2).
- Its Cat 4/5 bias gets worse (-5.0, p 0.02).
- Its predicted Cat 4/5-minus-Cat 3 gap, already inverted, widens the
  wrong way: -4.8 -> -9.6 kt.

The other storms:
- Beryl and Lee: Cat 4/5 lifts more than Cat 3 (+7.4 against +3.3 and
  +3.9), so their gaps widen in the right direction. That is partial
  separation.
- Sam: both bands lift together.

On the original three held-out storms alone, rotation looks clearly
harmful: overall MAE 9.72 -> 11.29 kt, Cat 4 bias -18.2 -> -23.2, Cat 5
-22.8 -> -27.7. As in the loss experiment, H3 and H6 point in opposite
directions, and Ian is the reason.

## Calibration check: shape, not ordering

Since rotation raised predictions at every intensity, each run was given a
linear correction, truth = a + b * prediction, fitted on that run's own
validation predictions only and applied to its H6 predictions; the primary
comparisons were then repeated with the same exact test and threshold
(`scripts/rotation_calibration.py`, `calibration_tests.csv`). Slopes were
the same in both arms (b 0.939 control, 0.937 rotation); intercepts were
+4.0 and +2.7.

| rotation - control (H6) | uncorrected | corrected | corrected / uncorrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| >96 kt bias | +4.67 (p 0.0028) | +2.86 (p 0.00004) | 61% |
| >96 kt MAE | -2.21 (p 0.019) | -1.68 (p 0.0008) | 76% |
| overall MAE | -0.49 (p 0.25) | -0.26 (p 0.27) | |

About 40% of the bias gain was a uniform shift that the correction
removes; the rest survives, and >96 kt MAE passes the threshold once both
arms are corrected. (Corrected p values are smaller because the correction
also removes most of the control arm's run-to-run spread, which was largely
calibration offset.)

But rotation does not rank strong storms better. Correlation is unchanged
by a linear correction, and within the >96 kt frames it did not improve:
Spearman 0.506 -> 0.517 (p 0.46), Pearson 0.520 -> 0.542 (p 0.10). Over
all H6 frames ranking got worse (Pearson 0.920 -> 0.905, p 0.0001), from
the weak bands. What survives the correction is the high end sitting
higher relative to everything else -- the upward shift grows from +2.0 kt
at TD to +7.3 at Cat 5 -- which one validation-fitted line cannot give the
control.

**Rotation changed the shape of the response at the high end, not the
ordering of frames.** The output saturates less at the top. Corrected, the
storms split as before: Beryl, Lee and Sam improve, Ian gets worse (>96 kt
bias -5.7 -> -9.5, p 0.0001).

## Run-to-run spread

Rotation runs are much more consistent than control runs. Standard
deviation over ten runs, control -> rotation:

| metric | control | rotation |
|---|---|---|
| H6 overall MAE | 1.24 | 0.48 |
| >96 kt bias | 3.52 | 2.45 |
| >96 kt MAE | 2.39 | 1.25 |

The masked control's H6 overall MAE falls into two groups (10.8-11.9 and
13.0-14.0 kt), so its sd is larger than the unmasked L1 arm's (0.57).

## Checks

- Integrity: every run had the intended mask and rotation flags, storms,
  frames and epochs; all storm-fraction values were computed on masked,
  unrotated frames.
- Run order: no pooled trend (Spearman +0.32, p 0.17). The first run in
  each round averaged 0.75 kt worse than the second (exact p 0.064). Arm
  order alternated, so each arm ran first five times, and this cannot
  bias the arm comparison.

## Limitations

- One augmentation, one angle distribution. Rotation also removes
  physically meaningful orientation: north-south asymmetry, the usual
  direction of shear, where land sits in the frame. Ian, the storm that
  got worse, is the one whose high-end frames sit near Cuba and Florida.
  That is an observation, not a tested explanation.
- The calibration check below rests on a validation set whose only frames
  above 96 kt are Laura's (25 frames, maximum 130 kt), so it tests linear
  recalibration only.
- 40 epochs for both arms. Augmentation can need more; the selected
  epochs do not suggest the rotation arm was cut short, but longer runs
  were not tried.
