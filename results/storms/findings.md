# More-distinct-storms experiment findings

Generated by `scripts/storms_analysis.py`, which was written, smoke-tested on
synthetic data and fixed before launch (its SHA-256 at launch, in
`logs/storms/analysis_sha256_at_launch.txt`, matches the file after the
run), from the per-sample prediction CSVs in this folder
(`<arm>_seed<s>_h9.csv`), the per-run storm-fraction files
(`<arm>_seed<s>_storm_fraction.json`) and the training logs in
`logs/storms/`. Per-run numbers in `summary_per_run.csv`, per-arm tables in
`summary_by_arm.csv`, the three primary tests in `primary_tests.csv`,
everything else in `exploratory_differences.csv`. The run-order robustness
check is `scripts/storms_robustness.py` -> `robustness_control_seeds0-4.csv`.
Runs made by `scripts/storms_experiment.sh`. Design, build, exclusions,
pre-launch and results entries in `logs/decisions.md` (2026-10-14). Only
each run's validation-selected checkpoint was kept
(`models/storms/<arm>/seed<s>.pt`). `best_model.pt` and
`models/baseline_9kt.pt` were not touched.

## Result: all three primary tests survive

Training on more distinct storms improved high-end ranking, reduced the
high-end underestimation, and lowered overall error on the nine held-out
storms. All three pre-registered tests survive correction, in the
improving direction.

| test (H9 pooled, expanded - control) | control | expanded | difference | exact p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spearman, frames > 96 kt | 0.523 (0.053) | 0.580 (0.024) | +0.057 | 0.0024 |
| >96 kt bias | -13.12 (2.01) | -7.72 (1.43) | +5.40 | 0.00003 |
| overall MAE | 10.87 (0.63) | 9.27 (0.29) | -1.60 | 0.00001 |

Mean (sd) over ten runs. Two-sided exact permutation test on the difference
of arm means (all 184,756 splits), Bonferroni threshold 0.05 / 3 = 0.0167.
This is the first intervention in this series to move ranking within the
high end; neither the loss functions nor rotation did. All ten expanded
runs have lower overall MAE than the best control run.

## The design cannot separate storm variety from data volume

Read this before reading the result as "variety helps". The expanded arm
differs from the control in three ways at once, at an equal step budget:

| | control | expanded |
|---|---|---|
| distinct training storms | 9 | 19 |
| training frames | 1307 | 2081 |
| training frames >= 96 kt | 258 | 394 |
| training frames >= 113 kt | 174 | 220 |

The improvement could come from more distinct storms, from more frames, or
from more high-end frames, and this experiment cannot tell those apart.
Adding Beryl and Lee hourly in the class-imbalance experiment (+509 frames,
+219 at >= 96 kt) did not help, which hints that variety matters more than
volume -- but that experiment used different held-out storms and is not
directly comparable. A variety-against-volume experiment, using only data
already on disk, is drafted in `logs/decisions.md`.

## Pre-registered secondary: null

The same-storm neighbour fraction, computed on the original nine storms'
1307 frames in each run's embedding (k = 20, same-storm frames within
+-6 h excluded; chance level identical in both arms), went from 0.365 to
0.369 (exact p 0.78). The gain did not come with a less storm-organized
embedding of those storms, so the mechanism the design proposed is not
supported. Whatever more storms changed, it was not this.

## Flags set in advance

**Possible under-training.** 6 of 10 expanded runs selected epoch 24 or 25
of 25 (control: epochs 19-40). By the pre-set rule this is flagged as
possibly under-trained and not rerun. If the expanded arm was cut short,
its gain is understated, not overstated.

**Run order.** In the control arm, H9 overall MAE rose with seed (Spearman
+0.72); the expanded arm was flat (+0.01). Pooled against run order, with
arm means removed, +0.44 (p 0.05); position within a round had no effect
(-0.00 kt, p 0.99). By the pre-set rule a trend in one arm only is read as
chance within that arm, but it is strong, and within an arm seed and order
cannot be separated. So the three comparisons were repeated against
control seeds 0-4 only -- its earlier and better runs -- as a robustness
check, not a new primary test (exact permutation over 3003 splits, same
0.0167):

| metric | control seeds 0-4 | expanded | difference | exact p | passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spearman, frames > 96 kt | 0.555 | 0.580 | +0.025 | 0.053 | **no** |
| >96 kt bias | -11.93 | -7.72 | +4.22 | 0.0010 | yes |
| overall MAE | 10.40 | 9.27 | -1.13 | 0.0003 | yes |

The bias and overall-MAE results hold against the better half of the
control. The ranking result does not: against those five runs the Spearman
gain halves and misses the threshold. The ranking improvement depends
partly on the control's later, worse runs, and should be treated as
suggestive rather than established.

## Weak-storm error is unchanged

Unlike rotation, which raised predictions everywhere and made weak storms
worse, more storms lifted only the bands that were under-predicted. On H9:
< 64 kt MAE 7.89 -> 7.84 (p 0.83); TD bias +7.4 -> +7.4; TS -0.4 -> -0.8.
Bias by band, control -> expanded (exploratory):

| band | control | expanded | shift (p) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat 1 | -5.2 | -2.3 | +2.9 (0.003) |
| Cat 2 | -6.1 | -2.6 | +3.5 (0.0001) |
| Cat 3 | -8.7 | -4.2 | +4.5 (< 0.0001) |
| Cat 4 | -15.8 | -9.5 | +6.3 (< 0.0001) |
| Cat 5 | -21.6 | -18.8 | +2.7 (0.07) |

Cat 5 underestimation remains at about -19 kt. Validation has no frames at
or above 137 kt, so epoch selection was blind at Cat 5, and Lorenzo, the
only new storm with a Cat 5 label, added a single frame.

## Per storm and the Ian check (exploratory)

>96 kt, control -> expanded, mean over ten runs:

| storm (>96 kt frames) | Spearman | MAE | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam (188) | 0.55 -> 0.64 (p 0.0009) | 16.8 -> 10.7 | -15.9 -> -8.8 |
| Fiona (89) | 0.54 -> 0.66 (p 0.0006) | 9.1 -> 6.4 | -7.8 -> -3.4 |
| Lee (115) | 0.41 -> 0.48 (p 0.06) | 13.4 -> 11.1 | -10.5 -> -5.5 |
| Kirk (78) | 0.82 -> 0.82 | 10.5 -> 6.3 | -9.5 -> -3.0 |
| Iota (32) | 0.69 -> 0.72 | 10.1 -> 6.2 | -9.0 -> -2.4 |
| Beryl (104) | 0.62 -> 0.60 (p 0.14) | 24.1 -> 21.3 | -23.4 -> -19.4 |
| Ian (47) | 0.03 -> 0.07 | 14.4 -> 14.5 | -4.6 -> -2.6 |

The mean of per-storm Spearman values went from 0.523 to 0.570 (p 0.0006),
mostly from Sam and Fiona.

Ian is over-predicted at Cat 3 and under-predicted at Cat 4/5, so it shows
whether a change separates the bands. It does not. Ian's Cat 3 and Cat 4/5
bias both rose by about 2 kt (neither significant), its predicted Cat
4/5-minus-Cat 3 gap stayed inverted (-2.8 -> -2.7 kt), and within Ian the
model still has essentially no ranking above 96 kt. Fiona's gap widened
(+2.9 kt, p 0.02); the other storms moved as a block. On the original
three held-out storms alone, overall MAE improved slightly (-0.52 kt,
p 0.036) and nothing changed above 96 kt, because there that is Ian only.

## Design

| arm | train list | storms | frames | epochs x batches | steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | `storms_train_control2.txt` | 9 (original, hourly) | 1307 | 40 x 41 | 1640 |
| expanded | `storms_train_expanded.txt` | 19 (+10 new, 3-hourly) | 2081 | 25 x 66 | 1650 |

New training storms: Florence 2018, Humberto 2019, Lorenzo 2019, Delta
2020, Eta 2020, Larry 2021, Earl 2022, Tammy 2023, Helene 2024, Rafael 2024.
Both arms: L1, unmasked, unrotated, ResNet-18 IMAGENET1K_V1, Adam lr 1e-4,
batch 32, ten seeds, arm order alternating each round, checkpoint selected
on the expanded validation set (`storms_val_expanded.txt`: Sally, Ophelia,
Laura, Teddy, Franklin; 940 frames, none at or above 137 kt). Held-out H9
(`storms_holdout_h9.txt`): H6 plus Fiona, Kirk and Iota, 1752 frames, 653
above 96 kt from seven storms. The control is not identical to earlier L1
runs, because validation changed. All storms are GOES-16 (2018-2024); the
2025 GOES-19 storms are reserved as a future cross-satellite test set.

## Limitations

- Variety and volume are confounded (above).
- The ranking result is not robust to the control's run-order trend
  (above).
- One validation set, blind at Cat 5; one step budget, which may have cut
  the expanded arm short.
- The original nine storms are sampled hourly and the new ten 3-hourly, so
  per-storm weighting differs within the expanded arm (Dorian 17.2%,
  largest new storm about 7%).
