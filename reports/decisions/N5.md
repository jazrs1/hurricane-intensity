## What it tests

Starting from the frozen expanded configuration, does adding every remaining usable GOES-16 Atlantic hurricane (31 storms, 3-hourly) change H9 error at equal training steps?
- **Arms:**
  - control: the 19 expanded training storms, 25 epochs = 1650 steps;
  - atlantic: 50 storms, about 4072 frames, 13 epochs ≈ 1664 steps.
- **Seeds:** 0–9 per arm, paired, interleaved with arm order alternating, all 20 runs under one commit.
- **Control:** retrained, per your O1 lean; hc-adversary agrees.
- **Checkpoint selection:** on storms_val_expanded.txt only.
- **Per-epoch traces:** validation only. No held-out storm is evaluated per epoch.
- **Marco frames:** dropped by box overlap with a 650 km margin (4 frames), chosen from the built manifest.

## Confounds, stated up front (design §0)

This tests a bundle, not storm count. No unused Atlantic hurricanes are left to match against. Between arms:
- storms go 19 → 50 and frames 2081 → about 4072;
- 1912 of the about 2002 new frames are below 83 kt, so the ≥96 kt share falls from 18.9% to 9.9%;
- absolute high-end exposure halves (9850 → about 5226 presentations), and every existing storm is seen half as often;
- the hourly-frame share falls from 63% to 32%.

A "worse" result is not evidence that the added storms are harmful.

## Primary tests (your additions applied)

There are two named primaries on H9 pooled (1752 frames), per member. Each uses a paired exact sign-flip test, two-sided, **Bonferroni α = 0.025 each**, with 97.5% CIs by test inversion. All of this is in code.

| Primary | Better means | Threshold (smallest effect of interest) | Power at n = 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| H9 overall MAE | atlantic − control < 0 | 0.9 kt | **71–100% (plan on 71%)** |
| H9 >96 kt bias (653 frames) | less under-prediction | 3.0 kt | **71–95% (plan on 71%)** |

The sign-crossing rule is fixed in advance.

**Verdicts are fixed in advance**, per primary:
- BETTER;
- WORSE;
- SIGNIFICANT BUT BELOW SESOI;
- NULL (informative: the CI lies inside ±SESOI);
- NULL (inconclusive).

Plus joint verdicts:
- TRADE-OFF: MAE better, bias worse;
- REVERSE TRADE-OFF;
- BOTH;
- one-only.

A BUDGET-LIMITED qualifier applies if ≥ 5 of 10 runs in either arm select within the last 15% of steps. Such a result is reported as is, not rerun.

**Descriptive:**
- per storm (>96 stats only where a storm has ≥ 10 frames above 96 kt);
- original-3 vs added-6;
- per band, Spearman, and the ensemble mean;
- the frozen members vs the retrained control (unpaired).

## The open choice: matched grid (recommended) vs native selection

At 13 epochs the atlantic arm has 13 candidate checkpoints, while the control natively has 25. A coarser grid biases against the atlantic arm, by about +0.13 kt at selection on a validation-only proxy.
- **Matched grid (recommended):**
  - the control saves every epoch's weights;
  - its checkpoint is re-selected on validation only from the odd epochs (13 candidates, about 132 vs 128 steps apart);
  - only that checkpoint goes to H9;
  - the native selection is a pre-registered sensitivity check, costing one extra H9 evaluation per seed.
  - It costs about 5.9 GB of temporary disk until the analysis runs.
- **Native:** each arm as train.py normally selects. Simpler, but it carries the known bias against the atlantic arm, and it answers "each arm as normally trained" rather than the design's question.
- **hc-adversary:** recommends the matched grid. It removes a bias of known direction, compares like with like, and adds no held-out exposure.

## How the analysis is protected (hc-adversary items 5 / 7 / 7b)

`analyze_n5.py` refuses:
- any 2025 or non-H9 storm;
- any input not at its pinned path;
- any CSV whose MAE, rows and path don't match its evaluate.py log;
- identical prediction vectors across runs, except a control seed whose native pick is an odd epoch, where matched = native must be identical;
- a training log whose storm set, frame counts, epochs or settings differ from the design;
- UTF-16 or BOM logs;
- any working copy that differs from the committed blob.

The script's hash is the committed blob's, computed in Python, because autocrlf makes Windows working copies differ.

Each attack hc-adversary demonstrated is now a test that must be refused:
- the same CSVs for both arms;
- CSVs swapped between arms (this flipped the verdict in r2);
- a CSV pointed at another arm's file;
- an eval log at an arbitrary path;
- a fake training storm set.
