## Item 3: BETTER and "a null is the expected outcome"

**What BETTER means (§5, as recommended and decided):** v2 is significantly better (p < 0.05), and the CI **reaches** beyond −1.0 kt. The CI does **not** have to lie wholly beyond −1.0 kt.

All figures below come from hc-adversary's simulation. It uses a t-approximation to the exact sign-flip test, which reproduces the protocol's power table, so the figures are approximate.

**Readings if v2's true gain is 0.48 kt (your figure):**

| n | sd | BETTER (replace) | SIGNIFICANT BUT BELOW SESOI | NULL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.5 | 0.03 | 0.92 | 0.05 |
| 16 | 1.0 | 0.35 | 0.08 | 0.57 |
| 16 | 1.5 | 0.22 | 0.00 | 0.78 |
| 23 | 1.0 | 0.32 | 0.28 | 0.41 |
| 23 | 1.5 | 0.30 | 0.01 | 0.69 |

**What it means:**
- At sd about 0.5, the likely reading is SIGNIFICANT BUT BELOW SESOI, not NULL.
- At sd ≥ 1, NULL is the most common single reading (0.41–0.78), but not always a majority. So your "a null is the expected outcome" holds only if sd ≥ 1, and then only as the most common reading.
- In most outcomes the frozen ensemble stays.
- But at sd 1 to 1.5, a true 0.48 kt gain still reads BETTER, and replaces the ensemble, about 1 time in 3 to 1 in 5.

**Three ways to define BETTER.** Each column gives the chance of replacement.

| v2's true gain | (a) as decided: p < 0.05 and the CI **reaches** beyond −1.0 | (b) the CI lies **wholly** beyond −1.0 | (c) p < 0.05 and d̄ ≤ −1.0 kt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (no better) | ≤ 0.024 | about 0 | ≤ 0.004 |
| 0.48 kt | 0.22–0.35 at sd ≥ 1 (0.03 at sd 0.5) | ≤ 0.001 | 0.00–0.08 |
| 1.0 kt | 0.70–0.97 at sd ≥ 1; 1.00 at sd 0.5 (the power table) | about 0.025 | about 0.50 |
| 1.5 kt | — | 0.96 (sd 0.5); 0.47–0.63 (sd 1.0); 0.24–0.33 (sd 1.5) | 0.91–1.00 |
| 2.0 kt | — | 0.70–1.00 | — |

**The question each rule answers:**
- (a): "Is v2 better, and could the gain plausibly be ≥ 1.0 kt?"
- (b): "Is the gain credibly ≥ 1.0 kt?" In effect it raises the bar to a true gain of about 1.5–2 kt. A real 1.0 kt gain would replace only about 1 time in 40.
- (c): "Is v2 better, with the best estimate ≥ 1.0 kt?" A real 1.0 kt gain replaces about half the time, and a 0.48 kt gain at most 8%.

**Options:**
- **(a) Keep BETTER as decided** (recommended). Add a protocol note beside your sentence, which stays as you wrote it:
  > "Expected outcome: not BETTER, so no replacement. The likely reading is SIGNIFICANT BUT BELOW SESOI if sd ≈ 0.5; at sd ≥ 1, NULL is the most common reading. Under the decided rule, a true 0.48 kt gain still reads BETTER about 1 time in 3 to 1 in 5 at sd 1–1.5 (n 16–23)."
  - **Why:**
    - BETTER needs p < 0.05 with v2 ahead. If v2 is in truth no better, it replaces at most about 2.5% of the time, and almost never if v2 is worse.
    - A false switch costs the switch itself (new pins, a new site build, a new reference figure), not a worse model on the primary.
    - It finds a real 1.0 kt gain most of the time.
    - It keeps the N5 and F-31 reading categories, so the results stay comparable.
- **(b) Tighten to "CI wholly beyond −1.0".** Almost no switch on a sub-1.0 kt gain, but almost none on a real 1.0 kt gain either. It departs from the N5 and F-31 categories.
- **(c) Require d̄ ≤ −1.0 kt, as well as p < 0.05.** In between: few switches on a 0.48 kt gain, about half on a real 1.0 kt gain. It also departs from the N5 and F-31 categories.

**A caveat that holds under every option:**
- "Better" and "worse" here mean the primary: mean per-storm MAE, over mostly weak storms.
- The > 96 kt questions are descriptive only, because fewer than 6 qualifying storms is likely (protocol, Read first).
- So a BETTER reading could install a model that is worse at the high end, where the frozen ensemble's known weakness lies, without any test seeing it.
