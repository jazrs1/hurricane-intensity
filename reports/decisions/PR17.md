## Where the bug lives

1. **`src/hurricane_intensity/pipeline.py:46`, `build_sample_grid`.** It interpolates raw longitude with `np.interp`, so a step from 179°E to 179°W goes the long way, through 0°. That wrong longitude then becomes:
   - the crop centre (`:159`): a crossing frame is cropped in the wrong place, or comes out as an empty 0×0 crop;
   - the longitude in the npz (`:178`) and in the manifest (`:138`).
2. **`scripts/concurrency.py:34`, `position_at`:** the same linear interpolation of longitude.
3. **`scripts/concurrency.py:22`, `boxes_overlap`:** the longitude difference isn't wrapped, so two storms about 21 km apart either side of 180° never overlap.

**Correct today and pinned by tests:**
- HURDAT2 E/W parsing (`hurdat2.py:144`);
- the crop-box geometry (PROJ normalises longitude, so it is wrap-safe once the centre is right);
- `dataset.py` doesn't use longitude.

**Stale comments to update with the fix:** `pipeline.py:31-33`, `scripts/check.py:77-78` and `:83`.

## Choices for your fix

1. **Interpolation rule.** Recommendation (coordinator, reviewer and adversary): **the short way round, linear in time.** It is identical to today's behaviour wherever no step crosses 180°, so all Atlantic data are unaffected.
   - Great-circle interpolation would move every interpolated Atlantic frame slightly, so existing crops would no longer rebuild byte-for-byte. It also fails your "non-crossing East Pacific track unchanged" requirement.
   - The gain is negligible: about 85 m on a crossing step, against a ~2 km pixel and a 600 km box.
2. **Saved longitude range.** After the fix, should saved longitudes stay within [−180, 180), or may they read e.g. −181.8 just past the crossing?
   - Everything downstream tolerates either (PROJ, the fixed overlap check).
   - **Recommendation: re-wrap to [−180, 180),** so manifests stay in the conventional range. If you agree, hc-reviewer adds one range assertion. That also catches the last wrong convention.
